The old workflow of printing a PDF, signing it by hand, and scanning it back is wasteful and slow. With modern browser technology, you can sign any PDF document in seconds, directly in your browser, with no printing, no scanning, and no software installation required.

This guide explains the different types of PDF signatures, when each applies, and how to add your signature to a PDF for free.

## Types of PDF Signatures

Understanding the difference between signature types helps you choose the right approach for your situation.

### 1. Image-Based Signature (Most Common)
You draw, type, or upload a signature image that is placed as a visual overlay on the PDF page. This is what most free signature tools offer. It creates a visual representation of your signature.

**Best for:** Internal approvals, agreements with people you know, personal documents, and any situation where the visual signature is sufficient.

**Limitation:** This type of signature does not cryptographically verify your identity. Anyone could draw the same signature.

### 2. Digital Signature (Cryptographic)
A cryptographic signature binds your identity, verified by a Certificate Authority, to the document. The PDF records a mathematical fingerprint of the document at signing time. If anyone changes even one character after signing, the signature becomes invalid.

**Best for:** Legal contracts with high liability, government documents, regulated industries.

**Limitation:** Requires a digital certificate issued by a Certificate Authority, which typically costs money. Not needed for everyday use.

### 3. Electronic Signature Platform (DocuSign, Adobe Sign, etc.)
Cloud-based platforms add audit trails, timestamping, and email confirmation. They are essentially image-based signatures with a service layer that provides legal evidence of who signed and when.

**Best for:** Contracts with external parties where an audit trail matters.

**Cost:** Usually subscription-based.

For the vast majority of everyday signing, employment forms, NDAs, rental agreements, consent forms, parental permission slips, an image-based signature is entirely sufficient and legally recognized in most jurisdictions.

## Tips for a Professional-Looking Signature

**Use a transparent background.** If uploading a signature image, use PNG with a transparent background so it blends naturally onto the document without a white box around it.

**Match the scale to the document.** A signature that is too large looks amateurish. Scale it to roughly the size of a handwritten signature, about 2-3 cm high.

**Position precisely.** Most signature lines in documents have a designated space. Zoom in to position the signature accurately on the line.

**Sign in the right color.** Traditionally, signatures in blue or black are standard. Avoid red or other colors unless the document specifically requires it.

## What About Legal Validity?

In most countries, an electronic signature (image placed on a document) is legally valid for most purposes. The EU eIDAS Regulation, the US ESIGN Act, and UETA all recognize electronic signatures.

However, some document types explicitly require a wet (physical) signature or a certified digital signature. These include:
- Wills and testamentary documents
- Court documents in some jurisdictions
- Notarized documents
- Some government forms

If you are unsure whether your document requires a specific type of signature, consult a legal professional.

## Privacy When Signing

Your signed PDF may contain sensitive personal and legal information. When you sign a PDF using a browser-based tool like LocalPDF, the document never leaves your device. The signing process, including your drawn or uploaded signature, happens entirely in JavaScript running in your browser tab.

Cloud-based signing platforms, by contrast, upload your document to their servers. This is usually fine for business purposes, but for highly personal documents, local processing offers additional privacy assurance.
