Watermarks serve several important purposes in professional document workflows: they mark documents as confidential, indicate draft status, assert ownership, or deter unauthorized reproduction. Adding a watermark to a PDF is straightforward, and you can do it for free without any software installation.

## What Is a PDF Watermark?

A watermark in a PDF is a semi-transparent text or image element overlaid on each page. Unlike a header or footer, which appear in the page margins, a watermark typically sits in the center of the page at a diagonal angle, visible through the main content.

Watermarks can be:
- **Text watermarks:** Words like "CONFIDENTIAL," "DRAFT," "COPY," or your company name
- **Image watermarks:** A logo, stamp, or signature image with transparency applied

## Common Use Cases for PDF Watermarks

**Confidentiality marking:** Legal, medical, and financial documents are often watermarked "CONFIDENTIAL" before distribution so recipients understand the handling requirements.

**Draft status:** Working documents shared for review are marked "DRAFT" to prevent recipients from treating preliminary content as final.

**Ownership assertion:** Adding your name or company logo as a watermark to published work discourages unauthorized redistribution.

**Print deterrence:** "DO NOT PRINT" watermarks remind recipients that certain documents are intended to be read electronically only.

**Version control:** Watermarking with a version number or date helps track which version a recipient has.

## Choosing the Right Watermark Settings

### For "CONFIDENTIAL" Documents
- Text: "CONFIDENTIAL" in capitals
- Opacity: 25-35% (visible but not distracting)
- Angle: 45--
- Color: Gray or red
- Size: Large enough to span most of the page width

### For Draft Review
- Text: "DRAFT" or "FOR REVIEW ONLY"
- Opacity: 20-30%
- Angle: 45--
- Color: Orange or blue
- Apply to all pages

### For Ownership/Branding
- Image: Company logo (PNG with transparent background)
- Opacity: 15-25%
- Position: Bottom right corner or center
- Size: 20-30% of page width

### For Printed Copies
- Text: "PRINTED COPY, DO NOT DISTRIBUTE" or "COPY"
- Opacity: 40-50% (needs to be clearly visible on print)
- Angle: 0-- or 45--

## Image Watermarks: Best Practices

**Use PNG with transparency.** A PNG with a transparent background blends naturally over document content. A JPEG watermark will have a white or colored rectangle behind it.

**Pre-adjust the image opacity externally if needed.** Most image editors (including free ones like GIMP) can reduce image opacity before you upload. This gives you more control over the final result.

**Test on a single page first.** Before applying to a 200-page document, apply to one page and review the result.

## Can Recipients Remove a Watermark?

A basic text or image watermark added as a page overlay can be removed by someone with the right PDF editing software. If your goal is strict enforcement rather than simply marking, consider:
- Password-protecting the PDF against editing after watermarking
- Using a digital watermarking system that embeds invisible metadata

For most practical purposes, marking drafts, indicating confidentiality, asserting ownership, a standard visible watermark is sufficient and appropriate.
