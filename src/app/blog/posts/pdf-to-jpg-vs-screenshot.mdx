You need a PDF as an image. You can either convert it to JPG using a proper tool or just take a screenshot. Both produce an image file - but the quality, use cases, and appropriate situations are very different. Here is what you need to know.

## What a PDF-to-JPG Conversion Actually Does

When you convert a PDF to JPG using a dedicated tool, the software renders each PDF page at a specified resolution - typically 150 to 300 DPI - and exports the result as a JPEG or PNG image file. The rendering process uses the actual PDF data: vector text, embedded fonts, and graphics are rendered cleanly at the target resolution.

The result is an image that accurately represents the PDF page at a controlled, consistent quality.

## What a Screenshot Does

A screenshot captures what your screen is currently displaying at your monitor's screen resolution, which is typically 96 DPI on standard displays and 192 DPI on high-DPI (Retina) displays.

Problems with screenshots of PDFs:

- **Resolution is fixed at screen resolution.** On a standard 1080p monitor, a screenshot of an A4 PDF at 100% zoom gives roughly 96 DPI - adequate for screen viewing but not for print or high-quality use.
- **UI chrome is captured.** Unless you crop carefully, screenshots include browser UI, scroll bars, and background.
- **Quality depends on zoom level.** If your PDF viewer is zoomed to 75%, your screenshot is at 75% of screen resolution. You have to manually manage zoom to get consistent output.
- **No automation.** Screenshotting 50 pages means 50 manual captures.
- **Color accuracy may vary.** Monitor color profiles affect screenshot colors differently than vector rendering.

## When a Screenshot Is Acceptable

Screenshots are fine in limited situations:

- You need a quick image of a single page for a chat or email and quality is not critical
- The PDF is just a few pages and you only need one or two
- You are annotating something in a presentation or document where pixel-perfect quality is not required
- The image will only be viewed on screen at small sizes

## When to Use a Proper PDF-to-JPG Tool

Use a dedicated converter when:

- Quality matters - print, professional presentations, social media graphics
- You need multiple pages converted consistently
- The output will be enlarged or cropped
- You need a specific DPI (for print: 300 DPI; for screen: 150 DPI)
- You are processing files in bulk

## JPG vs. PNG: Choosing the Right Image Format

When converting PDFs to images, you typically choose between JPG and PNG:

**JPG:** Smaller file size due to lossy compression. Best for pages with photographs and complex color. Avoid for text-heavy documents where compression artifacts are visible around text edges.

**PNG:** Larger file size but lossless compression. Best for text, diagrams, charts, line art, and anything with sharp edges. PNG preserves text clarity that JPG degrades.

**Rule of thumb:** Use PNG for documents with text or technical drawings. Use JPG for pages dominated by photographs.

## What Resolution Should You Choose?

| Use Case | Recommended DPI |
|---|---|
| Screen viewing, web, email | 96–150 DPI |
| Presentations | 150 DPI |
| Professional printing | 300 DPI |
| Large format printing | 300–600 DPI |

For email sharing or uploading to a website, 150 DPI gives excellent quality with manageable file sizes. For anything destined for print, always use 300 DPI.
